Burst structure

A burst is a run of calls that start less than 50 ms apart, the paper’s burst threshold. Star always sends its three reviewers together; Full mesh opens each wave with five calls (its worker pool), then refills one call at a time. Traffic is ON for milliseconds and OFF for seconds.

Each burst merged into one arrival at its first call, then the gaps between arrivals again. Full mesh stays bursty: its refills arrive a few hundred milliseconds apart, too far apart to merge.

More agents mean more peer messages per round (N×(N−1)), sent through a pool of five workers. The burst mode shrinks as the waves get longer, and the gaps fill in between.

Models

Goodness of fit

Three families fitted to the reasoning-mode gaps (over 50 ms) by maximum likelihood, as in the paper’s Table 3. Poisson arrivals would give exponential gaps; that fit is the worst for every topology. Log-normal has the lowest AIC everywhere but fits closely only for Sequential (KS D 0.027, against 0.08 and 0.09). The curves are drawn on the histograms above.

Subsampling robustness

With thousands of gaps, a KS test rejects every family. So the test is repeated on small samples: draw 500 random samples of 50, 100 and 200 gaps, refit each family to each sample, and count how often KS rejects it at α = 0.05. The exponential is rejected almost always; log-normal least often.

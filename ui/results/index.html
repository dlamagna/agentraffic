Towards Traffic Modelling of Multi-Agent Systems: The Role of Coordination Topology

What does the coordination topology do to the traffic an agent system sends?

Davide Lamagna, Albert Cabellos, Alberto Rodriguez-Natal, Gábor Rétvári, Berta Serracanta

Main findings

  • Fan-out makes bursts. Star and Full mesh send calls together: 53.3% and 38.2% of their gaps are under 50 ms. Sequential has 0.0%.
  • Poisson is rejected. Gaps are not exponential in any topology. The best candidate, log-normal, is close for Sequential (KS D 0.027) but not for Star (0.080) or Full mesh (0.089).
  • Same rate, different load. Star calls at Sequential's rate (0.207 vs 0.208 calls/s) but keeps 67% more calls in flight. Full mesh goes from 0.97 → 2.94 and sends 77% more bytes per second.

Why look at agent traffic

Agents break the link between users and requests: one task triggers an unknown number of LLM calls, timed by how the agents coordinate. That coordination topology is a design choice, studied for answer quality and token cost but not for the traffic it sends.

Example: the maths prompt
A school is designing a small amusement park with three rides. The first ride costs $500 to build and makes $20 per ride. The second ride costs $700 and makes $25 per ride. The third ride costs $900 and makes $30 per ride. The school has a budget of $2000 and wants to maximize revenue in 1 day if 100 students ride each ride at most once. Determine which rides to build and calculate the expected total revenue.
Runs
500 Sequential, 481 Star and 500 Full mesh (1,481 in all).
Model
Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on one vLLM server, on a single host.
What is an inter-arrival time?

An arrival is the start of one LLM call. The inter-arrival time (IAT) is the gap between two consecutive arrivals in a run's discussion stage, where the topology decides who talks when. IATs are what classical traffic models are built on (a Poisson process has exponential IATs), they do not depend on the average rate, and they show bursts directly: a gap under 50 ms counts as part of a burst.

Takeaways

Topology decides whether LLM calls arrive as a steady trickle or as bursts followed by waits, and the difference reaches the model server and the network.

What it means

  • Traffic shape is a design axis, next to answer quality and token cost.
  • Rate limits and autoscaling should know the topology. The same average rate can need very different headroom.
  • Report burst-sensitive metrics, not just mean rates.

Limitations

  • One framework, one small model, four task prompts.
  • A single host, so no real network delay.
  • Fixed topologies and no tool calls.

Future work

  • More models, frameworks and multi-host deployments.
  • Mixture models for bursts and reasoning gaps together.
  • A generative model for synthetic agent traffic.
